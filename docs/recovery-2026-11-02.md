## Reference sharing through pin tips

In Nolman, no reference for D18's separate neighboring drawing reaches the field
assignment stage. It is identified as LED18 in the paired source; the extractor
must not infer that name from ground truth. The initial one-text-per-symbol
assignment correctly gives D18 to its diode. A later fallback lets nearby pieces
share that text. It measures their full bounding boxes, including pins: the pin
tips touch, so the distance is zero, although the bodies are 6.35 mm apart.

The second drawing therefore also receives D18. Their five detected pins are
pooled by reference, and `_number_pins` correctly declines its two-pin numbering
rule. Both real diode terminals become unnumbered. This is a field-ownership
error upstream of numbering, not a reason to weaken the numbering safeguard.

The fallback now measures body-to-body distance, using the full box only where a
piece has no body graphics. It retains the existing `PIECE_GAP` tolerance.
Drawing-order ownership, explicit references, multi-unit names and repeated
printed labels remain under their existing rules. A body-piece positive control
and an independently drawn part touching only at a pin reproduce the distinction.
The latter fails at the starting commit and passes with the correction.

D18's two numbers are restored. The same fix helps SparkFun's Tsunami Super WAV
Trigger Qwiic and two Olimex designs. On Tsunami, C77 and C79 each pool four
unnumbered pins from separate bodies before the fix; afterward each retains its
own two pins at the same positions, recovering four correct numbered pins. At the intermediate stage it adds 27 correct
pins, removes 26 missing pins and removes one wrong pin across the six corpora.
The neighboring unnamed drawing stays unnamed; no designator is invented.

## Numbered pin continuations

Nolman U10 input 5 starts on its triangular body. Shape 1370 ends at
`(340.63667, 248.15601)`, where late shape 1503 continues collinearly to the wire
at `(338.09669, 248.15601)`. Both initially resemble pins. `_group` mistakes
1370 for a body stub; learned splitting subsequently removes the late extension
without restoring the real input. Input 10 has the same mechanism.

Reconstruction now requires all of the following evidence:

- An existing pin candidate actually starts on body graphics.
- A decimal pad-number text lies beside that candidate, under the existing number
  placement rules. This is an anchor, not a new numbering guess.
- The outward path consists of straight collinear segments, with no branch or
  intervening body contact, and ends on an actual wire.
- The terminal point does not land on another body's graphics.

Only a fully proven path changes the candidate. Short PDF fragments may join it;
a dangling extension, a branch, a connection into another body, or a candidate
without number evidence leaves the original detection unchanged. Existing line
width and geometry tolerances are reused. The original numbering stage reads
the printed number afterward.

The splitter also keeps the constituent lines of a surviving reconstructed pin
together, treating them as pin graphics rather than body. Otherwise it can discard
the continuation while retaining its extended endpoint outside the symbol bounds.
The regression checks the electrical net, retained drawing and bounds, including
a late drawing pass. No learned model or decision threshold changed.

Inputs 5 and 10 are recovered physically and connected to their wires. The stage
adds five correctly scored pins and three exact nets beyond the reference fix.
It does not recover hidden supply numbers or solve every missing U10 input.
Input 12 has a separate blocker: in the chosen reading, its body-to-tip segment
is wire 431, not a pin candidate. The original PDF draws that segment in the same
red as other pins, but the selected scene assigns it the wire role. Reconstructing
a continuation from an already detected pin cannot recover this missing root.
That role decision needs a separate, number-supported boundary check.
Positive cases with one or several fragments fail at the previous implementation;
branch, body, dangling and absent-number controls preserve their behavior.

## Per-pin changes and regressions

The fixed scorer's full ledger is retained, including changes that aggregate
figures obscure. In Nolman, relative to the starting commit, the aligned ledger
contains 27 missing-to-correct and 11 silent-wrong-to-correct transitions. It also
contains eight correct-to-silent-wrong, two missing-to-silent-wrong, one
correct-to-flagged-wrong and three correct-to-missing transitions.

Seven newly scored silent pins belong to U16's separate seven-pin `STM32_VDD`
fragment. Its actual predicted pin set and confidence are unchanged. After other
pins are recovered, a larger fragment wins the scorer's one-to-one match to the
same true net, leaving these seven unmatched. That is still an incomplete power
net; the new status is not evidence of a newly introduced physical short.
The scorer was not changed to remove this effect.

The remaining newly scored silent pins are C50.2, C12.2 and R83.1. C12/R83 acquire
numbers on already imperfect nets; C50 also participates in passive-end alignment.
R6.1, R7.1 and R8.2 become missing in the aligned ledger. R6/R7 retain their original
pin positions and two-pin raw nets; passive normalization changes their scored
identity. These outcomes remain included as regressions in the reported counts.
`pin-changes.json` retains every transition, rather than only net totals.

On Olimex A10/A20 Micro, MIC2.2 changes from flagged wrong to missing. Its correct
pin count is unchanged. On iMX233 Maxi, two missing pins become correct and an
existing silent wrong pin becomes flagged. Thus the reduction in total wrong pins
is not a claim that one additional short was electrically repaired, and the
silent-error improvement includes changed review coverage after recovery.

No confidence cap or warning rule was changed. EasyEDA review items per 100 pins
fall 16.38283 → 16.28065, while review precision falls 0.49688 → 0.49372. Nolman's
pin-addressed issues increase as more identified pins can carry existing issues;
its doubtful net count falls 50 → 39. These effects are reported separately from
the recovered pin/net assignments.

Representative additional investigations:

- **Olimex LCD 7TS:** the 40-pin connector geometry is detected as LCD_LIME2.
  Its shapes and pin lines are each drawn three times, with stacked names
  LCD_LIME2, LCD_LIME and LCD_CON. The alternate-name recovery rejects LCD_LIME
  because its designator-model probability is about 0.002, and LCD_CON has already
  been assigned as a value. Forty pins from each alternative remain missing.
  This is reference/value and repeated-drawing recovery, not absent connector
  geometry. No model threshold was lowered and no name-specific exception added.
- **SparkFun nRF52840 MDBT50Q:** U1 geometry and many pin names are detected, but
  the module's functional pin arrangement is not a consecutive numerical row.
  Some vector-decoded numbers are `2o`, `4o` and `5o`. They conflict with the indexed
  57-pin module and exceed its existing disagreement allowance, blocking the fit.
  Masking those tokens in a diagnostic copy yields one unique numbering across
  three placements and could assign 34 currently missing or different numbers.
  That is not a shipped fix: production must distinguish uncertain glyph decoding
  from intentional pad labels, and Eagle's current library policy preserves
  names when package numbers are not established. No guessed row sequence, blanket
  character replacement or library-policy relaxation was retained.
- **Olimex A20 SOM EVB:** the baseline lists 40 compound pin numbers on the named
  A20-SOM-EVB_PLATFORM part while 200 scored pins are missing. Repeated units and
  their reference ownership need a dedicated source-level investigation; a list
  of missing pins alone does not establish which units were never detected.

The confirmed OpenLog input mismatch is documented separately in
[the provenance review](openlog-provenance-2026-11-02.md), including its limited
impact estimate and a proposed correction for review. The mismatched design is
retained in every extraction result here. Dataset correction is not counted as
an algorithmic gain.

## Rejected and limited experiments

An unconstrained body-to-wire continuation trial increases Nolman's correct pins
from 404 to 413, but loses five component references (C26, C38, C41, C43 and Q10).
It lacks sufficient evidence to distinguish hidden-number body graphics from
electrical pins. That variant was removed. Requiring an adjacent printed number
preserves those component identities while retaining five additional correctly
scored pins. The previous phase's broad collinear-stub exceptions were not
reintroduced.

The first numbered reconstruction retains correct net endpoints but lets the
learned splitter discard continuation graphics. That bounds/ownership defect is
fixed by preserving the established pin identity through splitting. Its electrical
scores are not presented as a separate additional accuracy gain.

All experimental snapshots, logs and case captures are retained under distinct
names in the ignored output directory. No production dead code remains.

## Three highest-value next steps

1. Recover repeated connector units and alternative names using drawing-order,
   copy-count and reference/value evidence together. The Olimex cases expose
   substantially larger losses than changing a distance threshold can solve.
2. Carry glyph uncertainty into library-constrained recovery of printed pad
   numbers, with agreement across orientations/packages and strict controls for
   intentional nonnumeric labels. MDBT50Q supplies a concrete failure and a
   known geometric fit; arbitrary sequential numbering is unsuitable.
3. Reconcile pin/wire roles at symbol boundaries using printed pad numbers and
   local topology. U10.12 is a concrete missing-root example; the original red pin
   becomes a wire in the selected reading. Preserve true body strokes and prevent
   duplicate pins, then validate across all six grouped development corpora. The
   remaining split power nets also need physical repair rather than scorer changes.
